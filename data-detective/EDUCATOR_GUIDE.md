# Educator guide

## Purpose and prerequisites

Target: approximately age 15, with individual adaptation. Suggested time: 10–15 minutes. Learners should read percentages and basic tables; the interface defines correlation through patterns rather than requiring formula work.

Objectives: explain how an axis can change appearance without changing data; identify unequal recruitment; explain why a larger biased sample need not remove bias; distinguish association from causation; and assemble a claim with support and a limitation.

## Walkthrough

1. **Graph framing:** compare 0–100 with a narrow, clearly labelled range. Record that values and the 65.0% mean stay fixed. Discuss when zooming aids detail and when missing context can mislead.
2. **Sampling:** draw repeatedly with both methods at 20 and 100 cards. Inspect group composition as well as the headline percentage. Emphasise that the documented procedure, rather than an undesirable result, is what makes the lobby sample biased.
3. **Correlation:** compare everyone with the two prior-course groups. The deliberately constructed reversal creates a concrete third-variable explanation. Association alone does not prove cause.
4. **Verdict:** combine an accurate descriptive claim, evidence from both views and the observational limitation. The activity scores selections only; it makes no claim to understand free prose.

Allow keyboard or touch use and unlimited retry. For scaffolding, ask learners to say “what changed / what stayed” at each step. For extension, have learners sketch a fairer recruitment plan or invent two different honest captions for the narrow-axis chart. In co-play, one learner acts as headline editor and another as evidence checker, then swap roles.

Offline follow-up: print or redraw a simple chart twice with different axes. Under each, write its useful context and one possible misreading.

## Sources supporting the lesson

- [UK Government Analysis Function — Data visualisation: charts](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/data-visualisation-charts/) supports labelled axes, proportionate scales and chart choices that do not distort data.
- [OpenStax, Introductory Statistics 2e — Data, Sampling, and Variation in Data and Sampling](https://openstax.org/books/introductory-statistics-2e/pages/1-2-data-sampling-and-variation-in-data-and-sampling) supports population/sample distinctions, representative sampling and sampling variation.
- [OpenStax, Introductory Statistics 2e — The Regression Equation](https://openstax.org/books/introductory-statistics-2e/pages/12-2-the-regression-equation) supports interpreting association while keeping correlation distinct from causation.

These references support general content. Evidence Desk has not been tested with children and makes no claim of measured learning gains or universal suitability.
