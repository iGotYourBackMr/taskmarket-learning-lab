# Educator guide

## Objectives, prerequisites and timing

Learners identify producers and consumers, trace energy from sunlight and water through plants to animals, make a prediction, and explain how a condition changes a simplified meadow. A learner should be able to read short labels or work with a co-player. Suggested time is 10–15 minutes.

## Walkthrough

1. **Build:** choose meadow grass, clover, rabbit, grasshopper and red fox while rejecting two different-habitat distractors. The displayed arrow always means energy moves from food to consumer.
2. **Experiment:** begin with the healthy example or choose a dry spell or shady week. Change water/light, select a plant trend prediction, then advance one deterministic step. Bars, numbers, table and explanation all use the same state. Pause means no automatic stepping; every change is learner-triggered.
3. **Restore:** diagnose a new low-water, high-rabbit meadow. Adjust resources or included organisms, advance step by step and inspect the change log. A model-consistent recovery enables completion, while the copy avoids claiming a guaranteed intervention in nature.

Definitions: a producer makes food using light; a consumer gets energy by eating organisms; a food relationship shows energy flow; a model is a useful simplification. Adapt by preselecting organism roles, changing only one slider, reading the log aloud, or comparing printed before/after values.

Offline follow-up: draw one local producer and two consumers, add arrows from food to eater, predict one change, and circle at least one important factor the drawing leaves out.

## Model

All values are bounded between 0 and 10. Each step computes plant growth from current water and sunlight minus eating, herbivore change from current food minus ordinary loss and predation, and fox change from current prey minus ordinary loss. Every update uses the same prior state, so identical inputs/actions produce identical results. These are illustrative indices rather than scientific rates.

## Sources

- [National Geographic Society, Food Web](https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/food-web/) supports producer/consumer roles and consistent food-to-consumer energy-flow language.
- [USGS Ecosystems Mission Area](https://www.usgs.gov/mission-areas/ecosystems) supports treating ecosystems as interacting organisms and environmental conditions and avoiding single-factor certainty.

The activity does not diagnose understanding or predict a real meadow. Species responses are simplified and similar organisms can behave differently. Adult discussion should name omitted effects such as migration, soil, season, disease and competition.
