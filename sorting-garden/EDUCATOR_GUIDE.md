# Educator guide

## Purpose and prerequisites

Little Lantern Garden offers practice recognising circle, square and triangle, sorting by one visible property, and matching one to three objects to a collection. It assumes only that a child can indicate or tap a large choice. An adult should remain nearby. No reading, typing or prior number-symbol knowledge is required.

The three activities take roughly one to three minutes each. Stopping early is fine. This is practice, not an assessment and not a claim about universal age suitability.

## Walkthrough

1. **Shape match:** the first circle turn is demonstrated. Later turns vary the number of choices, the target size and its orientation while shape identity stays fixed. Wrong choices produce an edge/corner cue and unlimited retry.
2. **One-property sort:** each round states either shape or colour. Colour bins also use patterns/words and shape bins use outline symbols, so colour is never the only cue. Tap-select/tap-place is the only required interaction.
3. **Count:** the child or adult reveals each firefly once. Each reveal highlights exactly one object and optionally speaks the count. The child then chooses a jar with the matching visible collection. New arrangement supplies a transfer challenge.

Use Sound off whenever preferred. Start over clears in-memory state; no child data is stored. Every core action is a native button and can be reached with Tab and activated with Enter or Space.

## Adaptation and co-play

- Cover one choice or name only two choices when fewer alternatives help.
- Point to the outline for shape sorting or to the pattern plus colour name for colour sorting.
- Count slowly with one touch or gesture per highlighted firefly.
- Let the child watch, point or answer aloud while the adult taps.
- Repeat one activity and leave before the finish screen if attention shifts.

Offline follow-up: find one circular, two square and three triangular objects together, then sort a few safe household objects by either colour or shape, one rule at a time.

## Sources and model limits

- [NAEYC, Principles of Developmentally Appropriate Practice](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/position-statements/dap/principles) supports play-based, responsive practice, adult scaffolding and adapting experiences to the individual child.
- [Stanford DREME, Early Math Resources for Teacher Educators](https://prek-math-te.stanford.edu/) supports early opportunities to notice shapes, quantities, patterns and sorting through concrete activity and talk.

The app uses exact, authored rounds rather than a diagnostic or adaptive model. Shape categories are simplified to prototypical outlines. Colour labels are paired with shape/pattern cues but cannot model every form of colour vision. Speech synthesis quality and language depend on the device; visible content remains complete without it.
