# Educator guide

## Objectives, prerequisites and timing

Learners practise connecting numerals 0–10 to visible quantities, finding complements to ten, and modelling addition/subtraction stories within ten. Objects come before abstract equations. A child needs only to tap, point or direct a co-player; reading is optional because prompts can be spoken by a grown-up or browser voice. A focused session takes about 10–15 minutes.

## Walkthrough

1. **Build:** tap ten-frame slots to match 4, deliberately empty the frame for 0, then complete 10. A slot holds at most one counter.
2. **Make ten:** five authored bonds cover 7+3, 0+10, 5+5, 2+8 and 9+1. Blue shows the starting part and gold the added part. Add/Take buttons provide a non-drag, keyboard path.
3. **Stories:** six guided original situations include addition, subtraction, zero and ten. A seventh unfamiliar block story is the transfer challenge. The frame, picture, words and revealed equation describe the same quantities.

Small hint names a strategy while preserving the problem. Show a way models the counter action. Neither is labelled by learner ability. Wrong answers explain whether to count on, count back or inspect empty spaces; retry is unlimited.

Adapt by reading only the key sentence, hiding the written equation, using physical counters beside the screen, or completing fewer authored rounds. Co-players may handle every tap while the child points or answers aloud. No timer, lives or rank is used.

Offline follow-up: line up ten safe objects, hide some under a cloth, and use the visible part to reason about the hidden complement.

## Sources and limitations

- [National Council of Teachers of Mathematics, Principles and Standards: Number and Operations](https://www.nctm.org/Standards-and-Positions/Principles-and-Standards/Number-and-Operations/) supports representing and connecting quantities, number relationships and operations.
- [Stanford DREME, Early Math Resources for Teacher Educators](https://prek-math-te.stanford.edu/) supports concrete early-number activity, talk and adult scaffolding.

The app uses small authored examples and exact integer arithmetic, not a diagnostic or adaptive learning model. It cannot infer a learner's reasoning. Correctness depends only on the final count, never a particular slot arrangement. Speech quality depends on the device; visible content is complete without it.
